The game in half a minute.

A real playthrough, from the campus to mission control: the CubeSat takes shape, passes its vibration test and lifts off.

  • In the browser

    It runs on a computer and on a phone, with nothing to install.

  • Saves

    Your game stays saved in the browser you play in.

  • Players

    You play solo. Multiplayer missions arrive with the consortia.

  • Language

    The game text is in English for now.

The game in 5 steps. From idea to orbit.

Pick the mission

You negotiate the objectives with the stakeholders and set the requirements: science, budget and deadlines have to fit together.

Build the CubeSat

You fit instruments and subsystems one part at a time. Mass, power, data and cost change with every choice.

Pass the 3 tests

Vibration, balance and thermal vacuum are minigames. Faults have to show up on the ground.

Launch the mission

You pick the launcher by ΔV and payload capacity, then the countdown starts.

Fly the CubeSat in orbit

From Mission Control you follow the whole mission and decide what to do when the satellite drops into safe mode.

Go or
No-Go

Every launch comes down to this question. You answer it with your design's numbers: mass, power, ΔV and risk.

See the method

A game built on the NASA method.

  • Phases A to F

    The project moves through phases and passes the MCR, PDR, CDR and FRR reviews.

  • Components from NASA TechPort

    Instruments and subsystems point to their TechPort record.

  • Orbits with NASA GMAT

    GMAT R2025a works out ΔV, eclipses and ground-station contacts for Earth, the Moon, Mars and Venus.

  • CubeSat 101

    The path follows NASA's guide for first-time CubeSat developers.
